Königsberg's most famous citizen was known for his routine. Legend says neighbours set their clocks by his afternoon walks. Inside that orderly life was one of the boldest minds in history.

### A scientist first

Immanuel Kant was born in Königsberg in 1724 and rarely travelled far from it. Before his famous philosophy, he wrote about physics and astronomy. In 1755 he proposed that the Sun and planets formed from a slowly rotating cloud of gas and dust. Refined by Pierre-Simon Laplace, this “nebular hypothesis” remains the basis of how astronomers explain the birth of planetary systems.

### The limits of knowing

In 1781, Kant published the *Critique of Pure Reason*. One of its key ideas: we never experience the world exactly as it is. Our minds organize experience through built-in structures, such as space, time and cause and effect. Understanding those structures shows us where our knowledge is strong — and where it reaches its limits.

### Sapere aude

In 1784, Kant answered the question “What is Enlightenment?” His answer began with a challenge: Enlightenment is our way out of self-imposed immaturity — the inability to use our own understanding without another's guidance. His motto was *Sapere aude*: dare to know, or have the courage to use your own understanding.

### The toolkit

1. **Think for yourself.** Check sources, but form your own judgment.
2. **Know your lenses.** Every instrument, model and mind shapes what it sees. Ask how yours might mislead you.
3. **Test your rule.** Kant asked whether a principle would still make sense if everyone followed it. Engineers can ask the same of a design or a policy.
4. **Treat people as ends, not just means.** Good technology serves people; it does not merely use them.

### The practical lesson

Careful thinking is a skill, not a gift. It can be trained, like a muscle. Science and engineering depend on it, and so does a healthy society. Daring to know is not only brave; it is a daily discipline.

### Try it yourself

Pick a belief you hold about technology — for example, “AI will take most jobs.” Ask: What is my evidence? What would change my mind? Who would see this differently, and why? Write down one answer to each.
