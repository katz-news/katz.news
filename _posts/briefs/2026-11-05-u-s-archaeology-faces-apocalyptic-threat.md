America's history is on the chopping block. The Senate is expected to start considering legislation narrowing Section 106 of the 60-year-old National Historic Preservation Act, which requires archaeological reviews before federally funded projects break ground. Those reviews, mostly by private firms, produce 90% of the archaeological data collected in America each year. Backers say the rules delay roads, data centres and solar farms. A coalition of more than 1,000 organisations calls the bill apocalyptic: once a site is destroyed unstudied, part of America's story disappears forever. The verdict is history's hasty burial: streamlining the paperwork means bulldozing the past.
