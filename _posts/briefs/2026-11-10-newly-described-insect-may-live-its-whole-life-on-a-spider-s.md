Some tenants never pay rent. This one drinks the landlord's blood.

A newly described species of web-loving bug from China may spend its entire life on a spider's web. The insect, named Heissophila sinica, is a variety of web-loving true bug — tiny, carnivorous insects that crawl along silk threads, largely ignored by their comparatively immense arachnid hosts.

Haoyang Xiong of China Agricultural University in Beijing and his colleagues have studied web-loving bugs since the 2020s. They are rare, occur in low numbers and behave strangely: scattered across most tropical regions, yet nearly 80 per cent of species are each known from only a single location. During fieldwork in Fujian province in September 2025, Xiong found millimetre-long reddish bugs on a spider's web. Comparing their physical features with the few known web-loving species, the team described them as a new species — smaller than their closest relatives, which live mostly in South-East Asia.

The bugs exploit an ephemeral microhabitat: the web itself. They feed on the scraps left after the spider eats, and on web-dwelling mites. And when the spider moults, they gather round and drink its body fluids. They do not avoid the spider at all. On the contrary, says Xiong, they like to follow it about. The size difference may be what keeps them from being eaten.

The team found eggs, juveniles and adults on the web — suggesting the insects may live their whole lives there. Randall Schuh of the American Museum of Natural History praised the fieldwork, noting that none of it is straightforward.

Conclusion: the web is not a trap, but rather a home — a tiny, tenacious tenant that never leaves.
