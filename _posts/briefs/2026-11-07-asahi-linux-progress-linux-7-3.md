Linux on Apple Silicon just had an eventful few months, and it is no longer a hobbyist's side project.

Asahi Linux reports progress on kernel 7.3. Two major drivers — audio and the video decoder — are heading upstream, and the video decoder took barely three months, the fastest turnaround yet. Initial support for M4 Pro, M4 Max and even M5 has landed. M3 support is now generally available.

The headline act is Thunderbolt. Promised in January 2024 "within the year", it finally arrived. Apple built its own controller, and Linux's Thunderbolt code assumed Intel hardware. Only USB3, XDomain and Thunderbolt Networking work so far. Docks, sadly, must wait.

Then there is the fun part. Retail PC games now run: Star Wars Battlefront and Wolfenstein: The New Order work essentially flawlessly. Grand Theft Auto IV and Fallout: New Vegas follow, patchily.

And the sting. Bad actors lifted code from the project's forks and republished it, exploiting the MIT licence. Asahi is tightening its licensing.

Conclusion: Linux on a Mac is not a curiosity, but rather a coming of age — fast, furious, and finally playing games.
