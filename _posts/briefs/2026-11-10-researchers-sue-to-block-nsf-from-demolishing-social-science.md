America's social scientists are taking the National Science Foundation to court. The charge: dismantling their discipline by stealth.

Several scientific societies and two researchers are suing the NSF to stop it from further dismantling its social science funding. The White House research budget for fiscal year 2027, which began on 1 October, called for eliminating the Directorate for Social, Behavioral and Economic Sciences — the SBE — which has funded more than 60 per cent of academic social science research in the United States.

But the demolition, the lawsuit says, is already under way. NSF has slashed new SBE grants by about 90 per cent, according to data from Grant Witness, a nonprofit watchdog. In many areas — sociology and anthropology among them — it made no new grants at all during fiscal year 2026, and it has no active funding opportunities. Researchers worry that when the 2026 funding extension bill runs out on 11 December, the directorate will be shuttered unless Congress intercedes.

The lawsuit, filed today in the federal district court for Massachusetts, asks a judge to compel NSF to resume funding social sciences through the SBE and to block the agency from fully dissolving the directorate. It claims NSF cannot do so without the approval of the National Science Board, which by law must be involved in decisions about changing the agency's composition. In April, President Donald Trump fired all 22 board members.

It also argues that Brian Stone, the chief of staff who has been performing the duties of NSF director since April 2025, when Sethuraman Panchanathan resigned, lacks the authority to eliminate the SBE.

Conclusion: the NSF is not trimming a budget, but rather deleting a discipline — and the courts are now being asked to stop it.
