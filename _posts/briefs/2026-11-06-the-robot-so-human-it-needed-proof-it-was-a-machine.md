Verdict: the robot on stage was real, and that was the surprise.

XPENG unveiled its next-generation IRON humanoid at AI Day on 6 November 2025. The female-form robot walked across the stage with such fluid, lifelike movement that the audience suspected a trick. Was someone inside the suit? Was it remote-controlled?

CEO He Xiaopeng answered with a single uncut video. No human operator. No person inside. IRON is fully autonomous, powered by proprietary neural networks.

The machine is engineered to look human. It moves with fluid grace and responds interactively. Beneath the human form sits a multi-modal AI system, built on large language models and computer vision, with a Turing-class chip processing over 100 trillion operations per second.

The unveiling matters because it marks XPENG's pivot from electric vehicles to broader AI. The company that built cars now builds colleagues.

The joke writes itself. A robot so human that humans demanded proof it was a machine. The proof was the CEO himself, on camera, in one uncut take.
