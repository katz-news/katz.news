Washington has stopped funding quantum research and started funding quantum proof.

The Department of Energy launched the Quantum Genesis Q Competition on 17 September, putting up to $215 million behind a single goal: the world's first fault-tolerant, scientifically relevant quantum computer. Applicants must propose machines with at least 100 logical qubits capable of hundreds of millions of fault-tolerant operations, plus scientific demonstrations in chemistry, materials, physics or applied mathematics.

The money is staged like a dare. Phase I pays up to $1.5 million per awardee for early milestones; Phase II splits a $100 million pool among those who demonstrate a first-generation machine, with two $50 million bonus pools for 150 and 200 logical qubits. Applications close 19 October — and only $2.5 million of the total is Fiscal Year 2026 money; the rest needs Congress.

Conclusion: the DOE is not buying quantum computers, but rather buying the moment they start doing science — and it has put a price on it.
