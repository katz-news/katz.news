AI will save the climate, says AI. The climate disagrees.

This week's Download opens in Antalya, Turkey, where countries are meeting for the United Nations climate conference. The host has called on countries to commit to ensuring AI is designed, powered and deployed in ways that support climate action. But in the issue's lead opinion, Mary Nichols — former chair of the California Air Resources Board — and Jeremy Tamanini argue the climate case for AI has not been made. AI companies like to make very optimistic claims about the technology's potential to help address climate change. The reality, they write, is far less promising: industries around the world are not doing nearly enough to harness the technology to actually drive down the greenhouse-gas emissions heating up the planet.

The second feature offers a cheaper kind of climate hope. Tropical forests are the planet's biggest terrestrial carbon sink, holding around 40 per cent of carbon stored on land. Forest ecologist Daisy Dent, a senior scientist at ETH Zurich, is part of an international network deciphering the animal and plant interactions that let some forests recover after destruction. The goal is a low-cost, high-impact alternative to typical carbon-crediting schemes, which plant huge areas with a single species of tree. Over a decade, her research has shown those large 'monocrop' programmes could be expensive mistakes — and that more resilient forests can rebound naturally in just a few decades, re-creating ecosystems that suck up vast quantities of carbon dioxide.

Then the must-reads, and the day's news is not gentle. Nvidia is investigating its own boss: Jensen Huang has admitted to intimacy with a suspected Chinese spy, and shares were down 5 per cent in pre-market trading. His apology to employees — 'Leading NVIDIA is the privilege of my life... I have fallen short of that responsibility' — is the quote of the day.

Democrats are planning a blitz of investigations into Big Tech, reportedly starting with Apple, Meta and Musk's companies, hoping to harness the data centre backlash to rein in the AI sector. The UN climate meeting is the first held after confirmation that the planet will exceed 1.5 degrees of warming. Tesla is axing the Cybertruck. Iran might attack subsea cables if the US escalates again. Chinese AI labs are struggling to poach Silicon Valley workers. Online piracy is back — this time driven as much by archivist zeal as by free content.

One more thing: AI is coming for music, too, with AI-generated works poised to suffuse our playlists whether or not we notice who — or what — made them.

Conclusion: the Download is not a news roundup, but rather a mood ring — climate promises unkept, forests quietly recovering, and a chipmaker's boss under investigation, all in one morning.
