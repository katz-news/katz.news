The 2026 Physics Nobel went to a man who turned a cubic kilometre of Antarctic ice into a telescope — and waited 38 years for the universe to answer.

Francis Halzen, 82, of the University of Wisconsin–Madison, won the prize alone on 6 October "for decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin". He first proposed using South Pole ice as a neutrino detector in 1988. IceCube, finished in 2011, threads more than 5,000 light sensors through a billion tonnes of ice, 2.5 kilometres deep, watching for the faint blue flashes of neutrinos colliding with ice.

In 2013 it caught the first neutrinos from beyond the solar system; in 2018 it traced some to a blazar, TXS 0506+056. Some 450 scientists at 58 institutions in 14 countries now work on the observatory. The prize is 12 million Swedish kronor.

Conclusion: Halzen did not build a telescope, but rather froze one — and the ghost particles, at last, showed up in the ice.
