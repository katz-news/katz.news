The Moon's south pole is where the first settlers will live. This mountain shows why.

The De Gerlache-Kocher massif rises about 4 kilometres above its surroundings, right at the lunar limb, photographed by Alejandro Sanz on 31 October through a favourable lunar wobble. It sits very close to the south pole, in a region of perfect contradictions: permanently shadowed crater floors that likely hide deposits of water ice, beside peaks that receive almost continuous sunlight for solar power.

Water and power, side by side, in permanent darkness and permanent daylight. That is why the massif is a candidate landing site for future crewed Artemis missions — and why the south pole is considered a prime location for the first human settlement on the Moon.

Conclusion: the Moon's edge is not a wasteland, but rather a waterfront — frozen water below, full sun above, and a future address.
