A magnet the width of a human hair is spinning at 138 million revolutions per minute. It is one of the fastest spinning objects ever made — and you could see it with the naked eye.

The trick is to remove almost everything that could slow it down. Researchers in Singapore placed a 30-micrometre sphere of neodymium, iron and boron inside a lead container, pumped out the air, and cooled the whole setup with liquid helium to about -269°C. At that temperature the lead becomes a superconductor, expelling magnetic fields and levitating the magnet through the Meissner effect. A magnetic coil set it spinning — and it barely slows down. It would take 17 days for its speed to halve. In the number of turns before it slows significantly, it is 'in the same league as the Earth's spinning'.

The fastest rotating human-made object is a nano-sized sphere, invisible to the eye and levitated by lasers. This one is visible, magnetic and cold — which makes it a superb sensor. It has already been tested as a gauge of vacuum at very low temperatures, handy for quantum computer builders. It could also sense magnetic fields, hunt for dark matter and listen for gravitational waves at frequencies no one has explored.

Conclusion: the magnet is not a toy, but rather a telescope for the tiny — a spinning, silent sensor that may hear the universe's quietest whispers.
