The cholera bacterium has done something good. It has inspired a glue that works underwater.

Until now, underwater adhesives mostly copied mussels and barnacles. Researchers at Yale looked elsewhere: at the biofilms of Vibrio cholerae, the pathogen behind cholera, which clings stubbornly to surfaces.

Their peptide, derived from the biofilm, sticks to a range of wet surfaces and glues them together. Tested with confocal microscopy, atomic force microscopy and lap shear tests, it held. It also clumps microspheres together, acting as a flocculant.

The uses are plain: closing wounds, repairing submarines, and any job where ordinary glue gives up the moment water appears.

Conclusion: the feared pathogen is not a villain here, but rather a teacher — tenacious, tireless, and suddenly useful.
