A deep voice is a weapon, not a love song. Audio-only studies said deep voices sound more attractive. But James E. Rutter and Lynda G. Boothroyd paired altered voices with full-body videos, and the advantage vanished. A deeper-than-average voice made a man seem stronger, better at fighting and more socially dominant, but not more attractive to women. Women disliked high voices rather than loving deep ones. Visuals mattered more than sound: what viewers saw explained 12 to 38 percent of their judgements, the voice just 1 to 5 percent. The verdict is vocally vexing: deep voices win fights, not hearts.
