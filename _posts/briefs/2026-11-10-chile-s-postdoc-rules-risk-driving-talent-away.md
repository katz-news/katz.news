Chile has built a door for young researchers — and locked it from the inside.

The Chilean National Research and Development Agency, ANID, has released the rules for its 2027 Postdoctoral Fellowship Programme, the country's main funding scheme for early-career researchers. For the first time, eligibility is restricted to Chilean citizens and foreign researchers who already hold permanent residency. Applicants intending to obtain residency after receiving the fellowship are excluded.

Read that backwards and the paradox appears. A fellowship exists to bring new researchers into a country. This one asks them to be settled there first — residency before research, roots before the job. A foreign early-career scientist who would have moved to Chile, built a lab, and brought her network with her must now already live there to qualify.

The risk is in the headline: talent, driven away. International researchers do not need a reason to choose another country. They need a reason to choose Chile, and this rule is the opposite of one.

Conclusion: the fellowship is not a welcome mat, but rather a members-only club — and the members are the ones who never needed the door.
