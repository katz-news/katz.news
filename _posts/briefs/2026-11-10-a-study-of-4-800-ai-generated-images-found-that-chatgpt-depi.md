ChatGPT thinks every farm is a postcard. Reality is a concrete floor.

An estimated 99 per cent of farm animals in the United States are raised in concentrated animal feeding operations — factory farms, where livestock live in densely packed, enclosed spaces. Yet when Kehan (Sky) Sheng of the University of British Columbia asked ChatGPT to depict dairy and pig farms, she got cows roaming on pasture, pigs rooting in mud, red barns and windmills.

The culprit is a hidden feature. Before DALL-E 3 generates an image, an internal language model rewrites the user's prompt, adding details the user never asked for. 'A dairy farm' became healthy cows grazing on lush green grass. The results, published in Technology in Society, are stark: all 4,800 images were analysed, and every dairy-farm image was pastoral, as were 99 per cent of the pig-farm images — even when researchers asked for realism.

The numbers tell the opposite story. Fewer than 3 per cent of lactating cows are raised primarily on pasture, and 87 per cent of pork production capacity is enclosed confinement with no outdoor access.

When researchers disabled prompt revision, the pictures changed: 60 per cent of dairy images showed cows indoors behind metal barriers, and 96 per cent of pig images showed pigs indoors on concrete floors.

The fix, Sheng says, is simple: ask clarifying questions, and show users the revised prompt. 'It's not like there's no fix. There are, and they know how. It's just not being implemented.'

Conclusion: the AI is not lying, but rather landscaping — a pastoral, polished, profoundly misleading picture of dinner.
