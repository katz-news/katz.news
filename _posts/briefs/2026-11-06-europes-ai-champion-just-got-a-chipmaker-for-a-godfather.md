Mistral AI is not a French OpenAI, but rather Europe's answer to the question of who owns the models.

On 9 September, the Paris lab closed a €1.7 billion Series C — about $2 billion — led by ASML, the Dutch maker of the extreme-ultraviolet lithography machines without which no advanced chip exists. ASML committed €1.3 billion and became Mistral's largest shareholder, at roughly 11%. The round valued Mistral at €11.7 billion, about $13.7 billion, more than double its $6 billion valuation a year earlier. Nvidia, DST Global, Andreessen Horowitz, Bpifrance, General Catalyst, Index and Lightspeed also took part.

This is not a financial investment, but rather an industrial one. A chipmaker does not buy into an AI lab for the dividend; it buys a customer, a partner and a hedge against a world in which the models are all American.

In the end, Mistral's pitch has always been sovereignty with open weights. Now sovereignty has a godfather who literally makes the machines.

Conclusion: Mistral is not a startup, but rather a strategic asset — a Parisian power play, paid for in euros and lithography.
