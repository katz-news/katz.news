Swift went out in a blaze of glory. Literally. NASA’s Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory re-entered Earth’s atmosphere on 4 November at 7:39 EST over North Sumatra, Indonesia, after nearly 22 years in service. It had hunted the fastest and most energetic explosions in the universe with X-ray and gamma-ray telescopes. A recent solar maximum dragged its orbit down. An innovative rescue mission to boost it failed. The farewell picture is a 2018 mosaic made from its first million images. The verdict is sweetly stellar: Swift died a shooting star, so its science can live forever.
