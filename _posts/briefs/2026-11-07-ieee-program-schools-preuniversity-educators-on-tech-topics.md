Teachers are going back to school, and this time the subject is the future. IEEE's TryEngineering programme, now in its third year, is schooling preuniversity teachers in artificial intelligence and semiconductor technology, so they can take it home to their classrooms.

Fifty-four educators took part this year, across the United States and in Cairo, learning the engineering design process and trying out classroom-tested lessons such as 'Making of a Microchip' and 'Train Your Tree'. The venues were the lesson: at the University of Florida, teachers toured a SkyWater Technology chip factory; at Arizona State, they walked through the NanoFab; at the University of Minnesota, they ran a photonics experiment at the Nano Center.

The logic is simple. A teacher who has held a chip understands a chip. A teacher who has built a tower challenge can set one. All sixteen Minnesota teachers now plan field trips to the Nano Center with their students.

Last year nearly 80 teachers attended across five US states and Mexico, with support from Onsemi. Workshops are now scheduled in Greece and Guatemala.

Conclusion: TryEngineering is not teaching teachers, but rather teaching the teachers to teach — a small, sharp shortcut from the lab to the classroom.
