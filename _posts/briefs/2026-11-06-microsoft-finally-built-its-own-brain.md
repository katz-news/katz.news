Microsoft has spent years renting intelligence. Now it is building its own.

On 28 August, Microsoft AI unveiled its first in-house models. MAI-Voice-1 is a speech model that generates a minute of expressive audio in under a second on a single GPU, and it already powers Copilot Daily and Podcasts. MAI-1-preview is the bigger statement: a mixture-of-experts foundation model, pre-trained and post-trained on roughly 15,000 NVIDIA H100 GPUs, now being tested in public on LMArena.

The models are not a divorce from OpenAI, but rather a declaration of independence. Microsoft remains OpenAI's largest backer, and Copilot will keep using outside models. But a company that owns the models, the compute and the product surface answers to nobody's roadmap but its own.

In the end, the question is not whether MAI-1-preview beats the frontier today. It is a first foundation model, trained end to end in-house, and firsts are judged by what they make possible, not by what they win on day one.

Conclusion: Microsoft is not a tenant in AI, but rather a landlord in training — homegrown, not hired.
