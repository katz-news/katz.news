A two-year mission that lasted 22. NASA's Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory has tumbled into the atmosphere, ending a career that supercharged the study of cosmic explosions. Launched in 2004, Swift pinned down the first short burst, traced most bursts to collapsing giant stars, and caught a supermassive black hole shredding a star. Its finest hour came in 2017, when it spotted the light from the first neutron-star merger heard in gravitational waves. More than 2,000 bursts, 1,400 supernovae and 7,000 papers. The verdict is brilliantly brief: a telescope built for two years outlived its mission eleven times over.
