The scoreboard disagreed with itself, and both sides were right.

At the International Olympiad in AI in Beijing, 2–9 August, some 300 schoolchildren from 61 countries competed in computer vision, machine learning and natural language processing. Russia led the unofficial medal standings with six golds, one silver and one bronze — eight medals from eight students.

Poland, meanwhile, topped the team leaderboard. Its teams took first and third place in the team competition, and Krzysztof Rojek of Wrocław topped the individual ranking with 542.05 points. Poland won seven medals in total: three gold, three silver, one bronze.

Officially, Russia won the medals. Unofficially, Poland won the event that matters most in AI: the team round, where students build systems together, including robots in a virtual environment. Individual brilliance is not teamwork, but rather its raw material.

Conclusion: the IOAI is not a maths test, but rather a rehearsal for the real AI economy — golden, grinding, and gloriously team-shaped.
