The Global Fund has hired its past to save its future. Firstly, the man. Mark Dybul led the Fund from 2013 to 2017, helped create PEPFAR, and ran America's global AIDS programme from 2006 to 2009. He returns in early 2027 for four years, succeeding Peter Sands. Officially, this is continuity; unofficially, it is a rescue mission with a familiar face. Secondly, the money. The Eighth Replenishment raised $12.68 billion against an $18 billion target. That leaves a $5.32 billion hole — and a fund that has saved 72 million lives since 2002 now has to do more with less (sorry, accountants). Thirdly, the plan. Dybul dismisses Geneva mergers. The Accra Reset says the future is country capacity: national plans, domestic financing, and a five-to-ten-year wind-down of duplicative functions. Mergers are office furniture; sovereignty is the building. Why does it matter? The tools have arrived — malaria vaccine and treatment, new HIV prevention — just as the money has thinned. A fund that once bought scale must now buy self-reliance. Conclusion: global health is not ending, but rather moving house — from Geneva to the countries themselves.
