California moved the school bell. Teenagers moved their alarm clocks.

A study in JAMA Network Open, co-led by Stony Brook's Lu Dong and RAND's Wendy Troxel, asked whether California's later school start times actually help teenagers sleep. Senate Bill 328, in force since July 2022, bars most public middle schools from starting before 8 am and high schools before 8:30 am.

The answer is 24 minutes. California middle and high school students reported about 24 minutes more sleep per school night than peers elsewhere — two hours across a school week. Across all ages 12 to 21, the gap was 38 minutes.

The extra sleep came from later lie-ins, not earlier bedtimes: California students woke about 29 minutes later on school days. Teenagers did not simply stay up later to cancel the gain.

One caveat: the study was a one-time survey with self-reported sleep times, so it shows a pattern, not proof.

Conclusion: the law is not a curfew, but rather a later bell — and teenagers, at last, are sleeping in.
