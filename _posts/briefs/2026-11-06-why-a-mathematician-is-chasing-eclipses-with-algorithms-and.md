He has photographed every total solar eclipse since 2008, and he has never enjoyed one. Miloslav Druckmüller, a Brno mathematician, chases eclipses to study the corona, which burns at a million degrees while the sun’s surface sits at 5,500. Coronagraphs cannot match an eclipse: light bends around their occulters, but the moon is exactly the right size. On 12 August 2026, from Pico Trigaza in Spain, his team had 107 seconds of totality, 20 cameras and an instrument that maps the corona’s temperature using iron ions. The verdict is brilliantly blinding: a mathematician, a moon and 107 seconds of darkness.
