Trust is not bought with scale, but rather earned with limits.

Dartmouth built NeuroBot TA, an AI teaching assistant for medical students. It answers only from vetted course materials. Textbooks, lecture slides, clinical guidelines. Nothing else.

The system uses retrieval-augmented generation, known as RAG. It anchors answers to specific sources. It refuses to invent what it cannot support.

190 students used it across two cohorts, in fall 2023 and fall 2024. 143 completed the final survey. More than a quarter praised its trust, reliability, convenience and speed, especially before exams. Nearly half called it a useful study aid.

Students trusted this curated tutor more than generic chatbots. Transparency did the work. Answers came from their own course, not from the whole internet.

The paradox is plain. A narrower tutor proved more useful. Students used it mainly for fact-checking, which surged before exams. They used it less for deep discussion.

Some users were frustrated by its limited scope. A small library can feel like a small world. Yet that limit is the point. Precision education means tailoring teaching to each learner, and trust follows evidence.

The next step is Socratic tutoring and spaced retrieval practice. The tutor will ask questions, not just answer them. Curated, cautious and credible beats confident and wrong.
