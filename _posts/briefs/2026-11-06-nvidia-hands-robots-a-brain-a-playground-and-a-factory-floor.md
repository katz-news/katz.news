NVIDIA stopped selling robots a single part. It sold them the whole workshop.

The verdict is simple. Robotics is not a hardware problem, but rather a training problem.

At GTC 2026, NVIDIA unveiled a full-stack platform built around Isaac GR00T N, an open vision-language-action model. Robots can see, understand instructions, and act on them. Developers can post-train the model for narrow jobs, from folding laundry to moving goods through a warehouse.

Data is the bottleneck. NVIDIA answered it with synthetic data.

Its Physical AI Data Factory Blueprint pairs Cosmos world models with OSMO, an orchestrator, to turn one real scene into thousands of variations. Isaac Teleop captures human demonstrations through headsets, trackers, and gloves. Isaac Lab 3.0 lets robots practise those lessons in parallel. Omniverse NuRec rebuilds scanned spaces as simulations for safe testing.

The machines themselves run on Jetson Thor and Jetson Orin, handling sensing and reasoning at the edge. A new safety system, NVIDIA Halos, adds guardrails from cloud training to real deployment.

Partners were already building on it. Agility, 1X, Hexagon Robotics, Wandelbots, and Cyngn showed working implementations.

NVIDIA is not building a robot. It is building the road every robot will travel: bold, broad, and built for production.
