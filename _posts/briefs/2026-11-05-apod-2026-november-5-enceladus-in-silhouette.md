A moon turned into a silhouette, and its secret spilled out. Enceladus, one of the Solar System’s most tantalising worlds, is backlit by the Sun in this Cassini image from 1 November 2009. The light reveals plumes continuously spewing into space from the south pole of Saturn’s 500-kilometre moon. Cassini discovered them in 2005, and they are likely connected to an ocean beneath the ice shell. The plumes feed Saturn’s outer, tenuous E ring and keep Enceladus’s surface as reflective as snow. The verdict is brilliantly backlit: a dark moon, lit from behind, giving away its hidden sea.
