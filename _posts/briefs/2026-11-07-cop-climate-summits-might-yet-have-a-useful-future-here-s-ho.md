The climate summit is not working. Everyone knows it, including the people who run it.

Next week, countries gather in Antalya, Turkey, for COP31. The format is the same as ever: arguments over funding, late-night showdowns over wording, and a final agreement that says too little. For decades, COP negotiators forged real deals, culminating in the Paris Agreement of 2015. Since then, they have struggled to agree on even mentioning fossil fuels.

But there is one bright spark. The Turkish hosts are touting an electrification pledge, and focusing on electrification is smart. Electricity is far more efficient than burning fuel. China and Indonesia are already ahead on electrifying their economies; India and Brazil are catching up fast. Richer countries, meanwhile, are not moving fast enough.

Conclusion: COPs have generated a lot of words and not much action — but a plug, properly pulled, might yet power progress.
