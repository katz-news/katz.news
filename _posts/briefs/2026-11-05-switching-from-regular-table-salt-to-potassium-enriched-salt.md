Salt, but with the sodium swapped out. Researchers from The George Institute for Global Health modelled switching the salt supply to potassium-enriched salt across 193 countries. They found almost three million deaths and ten million new cases of cardiovascular and kidney disease could be prevented globally each year. Potassium-enriched salt replaces some sodium chloride with potassium chloride, tackling both too much sodium and too little potassium at once. The verdict is simply seasoned: a full switch could avert one in ten cardiovascular deaths in most countries, though people with serious kidney disease need care.
