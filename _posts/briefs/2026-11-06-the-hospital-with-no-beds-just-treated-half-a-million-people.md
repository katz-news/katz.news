The hospital with no beds just treated half a million people.

Saudi Arabia's SEHA Virtual Hospital is the largest virtual hospital in the world, and it works by not being a hospital at all. It connects more than 240 hospitals through a single digital network, piping specialist expertise — intensive care, stroke care, radiology, more than a hundred subspecialties — into clinics that would otherwise wait weeks for a consultant. The World Bank, which highlighted SEHA in August as a model for digital healthcare, puts its reach at more than 597,000 people a year.

The numbers are the argument. Remote monitoring and teleconsultations have cut hospital visits by 35% among elderly and chronic patients. Stroke care arrives faster; heart-failure stays have shortened by up to 40%. After the earthquakes in Türkiye and Syria, SEHA even supported the Saudi Red Crescent remotely.

Conclusion: SEHA is not a hospital with a website, but rather a country that learned to practise medicine at a distance — and the distance, it turns out, was the point.
