Harvey has not closed its funding round. The market has already priced it like it has.

The legal AI startup is in advanced talks to raise at least $500 million at a $15.5 billion valuation, with Lightspeed Venture Partners set to lead — roughly 40% above the $11 billion mark it set in March. Annualised revenue has passed $350 million, up more than 80% from $190 million in January.

The product is unglamorous and lucrative: AI assistants that draft, review and research for law firms, built by founders Winston Weinberg, a former litigator, and Gabe Pereyra, a former DeepMind researcher. Five months after its last round, Harvey is worth nearly half as much again.

Conclusion: Harvey is not a law firm with software, but rather software that bills like a law firm — and investors are paying accordingly.
