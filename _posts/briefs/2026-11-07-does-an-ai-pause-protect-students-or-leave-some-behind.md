Two of America's biggest school districts have hit pause on AI. The children have not.

New York City Public Schools and Los Angeles Unified both paused classroom AI to keep students safe. The pause does not stop pupils using AI; it moves them to personal devices. More than half of teens who had an AI tool blocked at school simply switched to one at home, according to a Common Sense Media survey.

Yvette Renteria of Common Sense Media says the pause is the right call for now. Her Youth AI Safety Institute tested leading chatbots with simulated teen personas for months and found none yet valuable for children and learning. But she compares bans to abstinence-only sex education: teach AI literacy alongside the pause, or the lesson happens elsewhere.

Jaime Casap, formerly of Google, argues the opposite risk: treating all AI as one thing puts a reading tool in the same bucket as a chatbot. Evidence, not panic, should decide.

Conclusion: a pause is not a plan, but rather a postponement — and the students are already ahead of it.
