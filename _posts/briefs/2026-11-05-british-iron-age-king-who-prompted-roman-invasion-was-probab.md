The king was a queen. Verica convinced the Roman emperor Claudius to invade Britain in AD 43, starting the conquest. Now Miles Russell argues we have misread Verica’s gender. Iron Age names ending in 'a' were usually female, and Verica’s coins show female warriors on horseback. An inscription read as 'son of Commios' could equally be 'daughter of Commios', and 'rex' meant ruler. The idea fits new evidence: a 2025 DNA study found Iron Age women stayed in ancestral homes while men moved, suggesting status. The verdict is royally revealing: the king who changed Britain was a queen all along.
