Art hid in plain sight for 19,000 years. A close examination of a frequently visited cave wall in Spain revealed a spectacular assembly of rock art engravings, at least 19,000 years old. Josep Maria Vergès stumbled on the art at Cova de la Font Major, west of Barcelona, after tourists in wetsuits emerged from a nearby opening into a small side gallery. Among modern graffiti he noticed vertical lines, then many more. So far: 280 motifs, including 55 animal figures. The verdict is prehistorically plain: the cave was a popular tourist attraction all along, and nobody saw the gallery.
