For every Rs 100 India's best-connected founders raise, women get Rs 4. That is not a gap. It is a verdict.

Kalaari Capital's CXXO initiative published "The ₹4 Problem: Women Founders and the Market Gap Hiding in Plain Sight". For every Rs 100 raised by founders from India's most powerful startup networks, only Rs 4 goes to women. The report draws on macro ecosystem data — AISHE, NIRF, Tracxn, 2015 to 2025 — and insights from more than 140 founders, operators and investors.

The pipeline leaks at every level. Women are 38% of venture capital analysts, but only 16% at partner level. Women-led micro, small and medium enterprises face a credit gap exceeding $158 billion. And only 16% of total capital between January 2022 and October 2024 went to women co-founded startups.

The irony is documented, not assumed. BCG research finds women founders deliver higher cumulative revenue over five years. The market is leaving money on the table and calling it a pipeline problem.

The ₹4 problem, plainly put. Vani Kola, managing director and founder of Kalaari Capital, put her firm's name to the number.

Conclusion: the market is not missing women founders, but rather missing out on them — and the data say so, in rupees.
