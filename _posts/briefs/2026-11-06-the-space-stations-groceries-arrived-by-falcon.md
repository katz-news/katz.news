Space delivery has never looked so routine. That is the achievement.

Northrop Grumman's Cygnus XL cargo ship launched on 11 April 2026, at 7:41 a.m. EDT, aboard a SpaceX Falcon 9 from Cape Canaveral in Florida. It carried nearly 11,000 pounds of science, equipment, and crew supplies.

The ship is named S.S. Steven R. Nagel, for the astronaut who flew four shuttle missions and logged 723 hours in space. This was the second flight of the enlarged Cygnus XL, which carries 33 per cent more cargo than earlier versions.

Arrival took two days. Canadarm2 captured the craft on 13 April, and it was installed on the station's Unity module. The mission, known as NG-24 and NASA's CRS-24, was resupplying Expedition 74.

Cygnus XL does more than deliver. It can also reboost the station, firing its engine while docked to hold the outpost at the right altitude.

Since its first flight in 2013, Cygnus has delivered more than 158,000 pounds of cargo to the station. Each trip is ordinary now. That ordinariness is the point.

This is not a stunt, but rather a steadfast supply line. Eleven thousand pounds, on time, with no drama attached.
