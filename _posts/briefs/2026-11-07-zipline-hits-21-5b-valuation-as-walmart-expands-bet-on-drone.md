Walmart is betting that your next delivery will fall from the sky. Zipline just priced that bet at $21.5 billion.

The autonomous drone delivery startup raised $1.5 billion — led by Paradigm, WCM Investment Management and Layer Global, with Uber, Tiger Global, Fidelity, J.P. Morgan, ARK Invest and Salesforce Ventures joining — and signed a new multi-year agreement with Walmart to add 300 drone delivery sites across the United States. The pair have worked together since 2021, but only 21 sites are active today. Three hundred is a different business.

The money buys factories and people: Zipline plans to expand its US manufacturing facilities and hire more workers to build a nationwide drone delivery network.

The company began in 2014 delivering blood by drone in Rwanda, then expanded to Ghana and Nigeria. It entered the US in 2020, ferrying medical equipment to Novant Health in North Carolina, and won its Part 135 air carrier certification from the FAA in 2022. Its home-delivery Platform 2 drones carry up to eight pounds within a 10-mile radius; the larger Platform 1 flies 120 miles round trip.

The numbers suggest the bet is working. Zipline's share of delivery revenue grew from 10.8 per cent to 34.9 per cent between March and October this year.

Conclusion: Zipline is not a drone company, but rather a delivery network that happens to fly — a blood bank turned big-box bet, now worth billions.
