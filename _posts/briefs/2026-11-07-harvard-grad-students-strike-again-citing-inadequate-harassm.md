Harvard's graduate students are on strike again, and this time the issue is not just pay. It is protection.

The Harvard Graduate Students Union, representing 4,000 students, walked out on 27 October for the second time this year. The first strike, in April, lasted 40 days and brought Harvard back to the table. Talks stalled anyway.

The union's central demand is third-party arbitration for harassment cases. One in five student workers reports harassment, discrimination or bullying, yet Harvard handles complaints internally, and cases can take years, even decades, to resolve.

Pay is the second front. Salaries run from $26,000 to $50,000 a year, against a local living wage of $67,513. Harvard offered to lift the lowest to $31,362 by 2030.

The third demand is protection for noncitizen students, including notice when immigration agents enter campus. Harvard rejected it, and on 5 November its officials walked out of a bargaining session mid-discussion.

Conclusion: the university is not listening, but rather leaving — and its students have noticed.
