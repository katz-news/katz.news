Nominal tests hardware with AI, and investors just tested Nominal's valuation — it tripled in eight months.

The four-year-old, Austin-based startup builds an AI platform for engineering and testing complex hardware: aircraft, nuclear reactors, smart factories. On Monday it announced a $240 million Series C at a $3 billion valuation, led by Lightspeed Venture Partners and co-led by Insight Partners.

The numbers tell a tale of acceleration. In March, Nominal raised a $155 million Series B extension at a $1 billion valuation, led by Trae Stephens at Founders Fund — a 'preemptive' deal, meaning the investor made the offer before the startup asked. Eight months later, the valuation has tripled. Sequoia, General Catalyst and Lux Capital are also investors.

The customer list explains the enthusiasm. Nominal counts 80 commercial and government organisations, including Anduril, the defence company Stephens co-founded. In October, the Air Force selected it for a $71.2 million Strategic Funding Increase investment, a programme that requires matching funds from outside the Air Force.

Chief executive and co-founder Cameron McCord is a former US Navy submarine officer and an Anduril alumnus. Submarines, software, and a valuation that surfaces in eight months.

Conclusion: Nominal is not selling AI, but rather the proof that hardware works — tripled, triumphant, and thoroughly tested.
