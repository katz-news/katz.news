Give students an AI tutor and they will ignore it. Grade the conversation and they will think.

That is the lesson of 'One Click Away', a National Bureau of Economic Research paper (Philip Oreopoulos and Nina Low) that followed Khanmigo in 18 Tennessee middle schools for two years: 96% of students tried the AI tutor at least once, but the median student used it only a third of the days they had access to it, and the math gains were no better than Khan Academy practice without AI. Sal Khan's verdict: 'how much motivation matters'.

The author's fix is to make the conversation itself the assessment: in a small study with Aimée Skidmore (21 students, Collège du Léman, Switzerland), students were graded on how precisely they questioned the AI and whether they kept ownership of the thinking — 18 of 21 said their approach changed. In role-plays run through his AI Friction Labs, five students who retook a calculus exercise scored about 34 points higher the second time.

Conclusion: AI is not a tutor. It is an exam with a chat window.
