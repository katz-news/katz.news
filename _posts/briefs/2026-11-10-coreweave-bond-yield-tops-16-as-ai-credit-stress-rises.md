The AI boom is being paid for on a credit card — and the card company just raised the rate.

The yield on CoreWeave's 2032 bond, with a 9.625% coupon, has climbed above 16%. The neocloud's five-year credit default swaps spiked above 1,289 basis points on Thursday, past their October peak, before easing to around 1,155 on Friday. Debt markets, in other words, are charging the AI building boom more for the privilege of borrowing.

Jefferies' Christopher Wood put it as an analogy: 'Debt markets are sending a message to AI data center builders: we're hiking the interest rate on your credit card and may put some new curbs on your credit limit.' The most plausible end to the AI capex arms race, he wrote, 'is that the credit card will be taken away.'

The nerves are not confined to CoreWeave. The cost of insuring against default has risen across the board: Nvidia's CDS is up 76% in a month, Broadcom's 49%, Amazon's 44%. In absolute terms the spreads are still modest — roughly 139 basis points for Nvidia, 298 for Oracle, around 1,100 for CoreWeave — but the direction is unmistakable, and Deutsche Bank's Jim Reid warned that 'we've had plenty of warnings from the credit space'.

Politics is part of the price. The market is grappling with data centre spending delayed or scrapped under incoming Democratic governors in Ohio, Texas and Georgia, and moratoriums could hit 45% of planned US capacity, much of it in Texas.

Yet the money keeps flowing. Goldman counts five AI bond deals totalling $67 billion since 29 October, taking hyperscaler debt issuance this year to $296 billion. The risk is whether the spending earns its keep: hyperscalers collectively reported negative free cash flow in the third quarter.

Conclusion: the AI boom is not running out of money, but rather running up a bill — and the bill is now coming due, dearer by the day.
