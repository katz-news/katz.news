California has voted to spend $8.4 billion on immunology. The small print is the story.

Proposition 38, leading with 55.2% of the counted vote, authorises up to $8.4 billion in state bonds for immunology and immunotherapy research over 15 years. Supporters call it a response to federal research cuts under President Trump, and a route to treatments for cancer, Alzheimer's and Parkinson's.

Half the money goes to competitive grants. The other half — $4.2 billion — goes to a single nonprofit institute, chosen by criteria so specific that only one institution qualifies: the California Institute for Immunology & Immunotherapy, co-founded by billionaire Gary Michelson, who also funded most of the $72 million campaign behind the measure.

Critics call it a private project in public clothing. The institute had three employees as of September. Its defenders say a hub is exactly what discovery needs.

Conclusion: the ballot is not a blank cheque, but rather a billionaire's blueprint — bold, binding, and built for one address.
