Dragons are not real. That is the least interesting thing about them.

Nature has dug into its archive, fifty years back, to ask where dragons come from: the ecology and evolution of winged legendary beasts, filed alongside monkeys that show their behinds to their buddies. It is the odd couple of the week — mythical reptiles and very real primate etiquette — and both raise the same question: how much of nature is biology, and how much is story?

Fifty years on, the question still lands. Every culture invents a flying, fire-breathing lizard, then asks science to explain it. The archive does not settle the matter, but rather shows we have been asking for a very long time.

Conclusion: dragons are not zoology, but rather gossip with wings.
