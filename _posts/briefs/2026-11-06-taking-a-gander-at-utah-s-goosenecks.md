A river took the scenic route, then kept going. Tens of millions of years ago, the San Juan River in southern Utah flowed across a flat plain, like today’s Mississippi. Then the Colorado Plateau rose, starting around 20 million years ago, steepening the river and speeding its flow until it carved entrenched meanders through 300-million-year-old rock. The river runs through a canyon 1,000 feet deep, travelling 5.5 miles between two points 1.3 miles apart. The verdict is gloriously goosey: a river that refused to go straight, photographed from space and watched under dark skies.
