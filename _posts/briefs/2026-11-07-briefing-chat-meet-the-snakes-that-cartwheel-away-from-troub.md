Some snakes do not slither away from trouble. They cartwheel.

This week's Nature podcast meets the dwarf reed snakes of Borneo, which escape danger by rolling — an "active cartwheeling" move. Rolling is fast, but it has a flaw: a spinning body cannot keep its head still, and a snake that cannot see where it is going is in trouble.

Researchers filmed the snakes in the lab in Borneo in 2019 and 2022. The dwarf reed snakes, Pseudorabdion, solve the problem with precisely coordinated body movements, holding the head steady while the rear body rotates. Fast, furious rolling, in a creature with no legs and no wheels.

The episode has a second marvel: evidence of fossilised blood cells in a 115-million-year-old dinosaur.

Conclusion: evolution is not an engineer, but rather an improviser — and sometimes the improvisation is a somersault.
