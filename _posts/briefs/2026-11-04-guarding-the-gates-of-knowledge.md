Science has a gate, and AI has just walked through it wearing a lab coat.

AI can fake a plausible paper at the push of a button, and Harry Goldstein (Editor in Chief, IEEE Spectrum) asks whether detection tools and peer review can keep up. The gate is open! Benjamin Skuse (a freelance writer and doctor of applied mathematics) did it: “He basically hit ‘run experiment’ and ‘write paper.’ And then he didn't do a damn thing to it.” (Eliza Strickland, features and AI beat editor) — and was surprised at just how effortless it was.

IEEE's human-review processes and research-integrity checkers were the gatekeepers put to the test. Scholarly publishers are not the only ones holding the line: Wikipedia's WikiProject AI Cleanup, a volunteer effort founded by Ellia Lebleu, detects and deletes AI-generated material from the platform.

“Wikipedia is known as a repository of human-generated information, and they want to hold on to that,” says Strickland. “Who knows how long they'll be able to do that.” If the scientific record cannot be assumed trustworthy, the uncertainty delights quantum scientists and grifters who thrive where nothing is certain (sorry, facts). It is Jurassic Park, except the dinosaurs are footnotes and the park rangers are peer reviewers.

Conclusion: knowledge is not a library. It is a nightclub with a bouncer.
