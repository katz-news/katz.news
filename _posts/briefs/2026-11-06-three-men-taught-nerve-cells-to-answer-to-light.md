The 2026 Medicine Nobel went to three men who taught nerve cells to answer to light — and, in doing so, gave neuroscience its remote control.

Karl Deisseroth of Stanford, Peter Hegemann of Humboldt University Berlin and Georg Nagel of Würzburg won on 5 October "for their discoveries concerning light-gated ion channels and optogenetics". The story begins in a pond: Hegemann and Nagel found channelrhodopsin, a light-sensitive protein, in Chlamydomonas, a single-celled alga that swims towards light. In 2005, Deisseroth showed the protein could switch individual neurons on and off with pulses of blue light inside a living brain.

Since then optogenetics has let researchers trace memory, emotion, movement and disease to specific circuits — the beginning, the committee said, of a new era in neuroscience. The three share 12 million Swedish kronor.

Conclusion: optogenetics is not a treatment, but rather a torch — and for the first time, scientists can see the brain by switching it on.
