AI literacy is not about keeping up. It is about knowing when to walk away. At the Indian Community School in Wisconsin, children learn how AI works before they touch it: second-graders sort buttons to see how training data shapes a machine, and middle-schoolers weigh data sovereignty, privacy and the environmental cost. Wisconsin is becoming a data-centre hotspot, with facilities demanding up to 1.3 gigawatts and millions of gallons of fresh water. The verdict is thoughtfully thorny: AI must serve learning, not replace the struggle, relationships and judgment that make learning real.
