The best meat replacement is better meat. That is the logic behind blended meat — and the students agree.

In the autumn of 2025, Jeff Dodge served hungry students meatballs laced with mushrooms. They loved them: 92 per cent wanted them on the menu, and few guessed the secret ingredient. The product, Joyn's 50/Cut, blends plant proteins with real meat, and a wave of startups is doing the same. The aim is not to replace beef, but to shrink it — a climate fix you can eat.

The issue's second lead is medical. More than five million people have geographic atrophy, an advanced form of macular degeneration that kills retinal cells. A system called PRIMA pairs a retinal implant with camera-equipped glasses, and after training, more than 80 per cent of recipients could read, do crosswords and use their phones.

And the third is the agentic web. Tim Berners-Lee imagined it in 2001: an internet navigated by autonomous agents, no browsers required. A quarter-century on, AI agents are triaging inboxes, doing the shopping and accessing bank accounts. Whether the human web survives the transition is anyone's guess.

Elsewhere: Democrats dealt tech billionaires a midterm setback, Google is shutting off Search for schools that reject AI, Finland opened a nuclear waste vault built to last 100,000 years, and dinosaur blood was found in fossils for the first time.

Conclusion: the web is not ending, but rather being eaten — by blended beef, restored sight and agents that shop for you.
