A detector reads the ideas, not the words. IdeaLens, from the University of Maryland, strips a paper to a skeleton of concepts and asks whether the reasoning is human or machine. It mistook AI-written documents for human work just 1% of the time, and recognised AI-edited texts as human in about 95% of cases, though it missed 10% of AI work. About 12.5% of biomedical articles show signs of substantial AI involvement, and 100 hallucinated citations slipped past reviewers at NeurIPS 2025. The verdict is satisfyingly sloppy: human science is messy, and that mess is what gives it away.
