A humanoid robot cleaned an apartment in San Francisco. It was remotely piloted by a human, but the future still felt close. Grace Huckins signed up for Tau Robotics' cleaning service after seeing it on X, wanting her flat to feel like The Jetsons, even if just for an hour. The robot arrived, the humans drove it from afar through its cameras, and the glimpse of the future came with unexpected challenges.

Meanwhile, Samsung is building the hardware the future runs on. At its Pyeongtaek campus south of Seoul, three chip fabs already run around the clock, a fourth is coming online, and thousands of workers are racing to build a fifth — all to feed the demand for the memory chips generative AI needs.

And then the markets had their say. Almost $2.5 trillion was wiped off tech stocks, the Nasdaq's worst day since April 2025, amid fears of an AI slowdown. The Magnificent Seven lost over $1 trillion; Tesla and SpaceX shed more than $420 billion between them. 'The steamroller showed up today,' one wealth adviser told Reuters.

Elsewhere in the issue: Democrats lining up to rein in Big Tech, a cyber threat to commercial freezers, active volcanoes on Mars, NASA's mysterious X-70 aircraft, and the end of the Cybertruck.

Conclusion: the future is not arriving, but rather being assembled — by a robot with a remote control, a fab running all night, and a market that just lost $2.5 trillion.
