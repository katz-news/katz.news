Heatwaves are not just getting hotter. They are feeding on themselves.

New research in Science Advances, led by Kai Kornhuber, found a three-way feedback loop: high pressure heats the land, heat dries the soil, and dry soil then sustains the high pressure. Each element amplifies the others. The result is extreme heatwaves 10 per cent hotter than they would otherwise be — a factor climate models do not fully capture.

The evidence was already in the record books. Britain passed 40°C for the first time in 2022; the 2021 Pacific Northwest heatwave blew past every model projection.

Europe, Russia, North America, South America and Australia will cross the triggering threshold more and more often.

Conclusion: the soil is not a bystander, but rather a booster — and ten per cent, in a heatwave, is the difference between hot and historic.
