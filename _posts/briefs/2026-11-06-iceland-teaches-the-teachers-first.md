A whole country can move faster than a single classroom.

Anthropic and Iceland's Ministry of Education and Children launched one of the world's first national AI education pilots. Hundreds of teachers, from Reykjavik to the most remote villages, will gain access to Claude.

The pilot starts with teachers, not students. Lesson preparation comes first. Personalised plans, adapted materials, support on demand. The aim is time saved, not homework automated.

Claude recognises Icelandic and many other languages. Teachers can analyse complex texts and mathematical problems. The AI learns from each educator's methods and materials.

The minister's verdict is blunt. Artificial intelligence is here to stay. It develops at a tremendous pace. Iceland will harness its power while preventing harm.

This is a national experiment with a teacher-shaped centre. Training, resources and a dedicated support network arrive with the tool. The pilot will test how AI benefits schools, supports instruction and enhances learning.

Small nations have a strange advantage. They can decide quickly, deploy widely and learn publicly. Iceland is betting that a careful, countrywide trial teaches more than a thousand cautious pilots.

The lesson is simple. Give teachers the tool first. Let them shape it. Then watch what classrooms become.
