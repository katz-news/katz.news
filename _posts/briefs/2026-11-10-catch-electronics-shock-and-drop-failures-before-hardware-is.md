Drop tests come last. They are the most expensive lesson in electronics.

Mechanical shock failures are among the most disruptive and expensive challenges in electronics product development. The trouble is the timing: the failure is usually found during physical qualification testing, when design changes are costly, time-consuming, and constrained by frozen hardware. Traditional development leans on physical drop tests, tilt drops and ball impacts, governed by IEC 60068-2-27 and IEC 60068-2-31 — all near the end of the cycle, when a cracked solder joint or a fractured connector can send a finished design back to the drawing board.

Traditional simulation could spot the weakness earlier, but it demands specialist expertise that many engineering teams simply do not have.

The white paper's answer is guided virtual validation. Using Keysight Multiphysics, design engineers run system-level drop and impact simulations through a template-based workflow — no CAE specialist required. Standardised validation plans automate the complete drop orientation matrix in a single run, while built-in reporting delivers decision-ready results. The approach identifies shock-sensitive load paths and packaging weaknesses before any hardware is committed, and its predictions are checked by correlation with physical drop testing.

Conclusion: the drop test is not the enemy, but rather the last resort — and with the right template, the computer finds the fault before the floor does.
