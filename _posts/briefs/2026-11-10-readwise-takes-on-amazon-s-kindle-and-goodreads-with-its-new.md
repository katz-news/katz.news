A small reading app just declared war on two giants at once. Meet Readwise Books — part Kindle, part Goodreads, all nerve.

Readwise, the maker of a read-it-later app and a reading companion that tracks your highlights across services, has launched Readwise Books: an e-book store and reading tracker in one. The bootstrapped startup calls it 'a cross between Kindle and the Goodreads apps': a single place to buy, read and track books, with far richer stats than either — words read, pace, depth, immersion and sessions.

The store offers 'millions' of e-books from the big five publishers and others, initially in the US, with print books (fulfilled by Bookshop.org's distributor) and some audiobooks too. It arrives amid an explosion of reading apps hoping to dethrone Goodreads with book clubs, widgets and habit-tracking. But few indie developers have dared to take on Amazon's Kindle store, which holds an 83 per cent share of the market when Kindle+ (formerly Kindle Unlimited) is included.

Kindle's edge is its end-to-end solution, from hardware to tracking log. Readwise sells no e-readers; instead it builds community, and it tracks books outside its own ecosystem — sessions via Kindle's browser-based Cloud Reader, photos of physical books to mark progress, DRM-free EPUB uploads and text-to-speech.

Readwise likens its metrics to a 'Strava for reading'. The desire to read, the co-founders write, is 'laden with guilt' — but 'that which is measured is improved'. Users say the app simply led them to read more, without goals, streaks or scheduling.

It is also open where Goodreads is locked down: a public API and an MCP server let AI agents research recommendations or wire up a hackable e-reader. AI features like session recaps need publishers' permission; only HarperCollins has opted in so far.

The app is free, in beta, and invite-only unless you already subscribe.

Conclusion: Readwise is not a bookshop, but rather a scoreboard for story lovers — and the race is on.
