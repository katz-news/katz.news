Cutting red tape could cut emissions, or raise them. The Senate votes on 9 November on the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act, a plan to overhaul America’s slow permitting system. It is technology neutral, opening doors for fossil fuels and renewables alike, backed by the White House. Clean energy groups say it could unlock 350 gigawatts of new clean power. The Rhodium Group’s best case cuts economy-wide emissions 4 percent. But one model found emissions would have been higher had the bill existed in 2025. The verdict is permit-tingly paradoxical: faster building is not the same as cleaner building.
