Voters are not shortsighted. Analysts call voters myopic because they judge incumbents on the economy in the year before an election. But Markus Prior and Talbot M. Andrews found people prefer a steady upward trajectory. In surveys, participants rated four-year sequences and chose rising trends even when flat ones delivered more in total. On average, they needed flat income growth of 2.3% to pass up an improving trend averaging only 1.3%. Between 32% and 46% picked rising sequences that were worse in every way. The verdict is plainly progressive: voters will pay for progress, even when progress pays less.
