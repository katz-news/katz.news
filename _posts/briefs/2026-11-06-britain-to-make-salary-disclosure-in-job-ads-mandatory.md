Britain is about to make the salary the first thing you see, not the last thing you discover.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham announced on Thursday that employers will have to publish pay in job advertisements, as part of a push to cut the number of young people not in work, education or training. Legislation is coming to parliament soon, though no timeline was given.

The case is stark. Just over two in five employers always publish salary ranges; fewer than one in five always disclose specific salaries, according to the Chartered Institute of Personnel and Development. Nearly a million 16- to 24-year-olds — 13% — are not in employment, education or training, one of the highest shares in Europe.

Conclusion: a job advert is not a mystery, but rather a price tag — and Britain has decided the price goes on the front.
