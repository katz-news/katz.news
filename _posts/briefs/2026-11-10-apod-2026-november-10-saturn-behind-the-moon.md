Saturn vanished, and the Moon is the only suspect with an alibi.

Sometimes Saturn disappears. It never actually goes away — it just slips behind the Moon, in an event astronomers call an occultation. One was visible in early 2025 along a narrow band of Earth, from Greenland through central Europe and into northern Africa.

Roberto Ortu photographed it from Sardinia, Italy, after sunset, and the featured image is a digital fusion of three shots: Saturn just before it vanished, then the Moon in two different exposures. The bright dot near Saturn is not a moon of Saturn at all, but rather the star 85 Aquarii, sitting even further behind Saturn than Saturn sits behind the Moon.

Occultations are a game of alignment. Three bodies, one line of sight, and a planet that blinks out for a while, then returns as if nothing happened. The next time the Moon steps in front of Saturn, from somewhere on Earth, will be in 2031.

Conclusion: the Moon is not hiding Saturn, but rather borrowing it for a moment — a silent, sweeping disappearing act, with an encore in 2031.
