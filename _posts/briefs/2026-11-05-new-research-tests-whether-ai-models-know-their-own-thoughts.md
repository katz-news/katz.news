Do language models know what they are thinking? Anthropic's Jack Lindsey injected thoughts — denim, secrecy, vegetables — directly into Claude's neural networks and asked the models to detect them. In 20 percent of trials the newest Claude correctly flagged the injection and named the concept, while controls never reported one. Skeptics Kyle Mahowald and Harvey Lederman then showed the models could spot the tampering but not name it: they called the ability anomaly detection, not introspection. The verdict is mind-bendingly murky: models can notice something odd inside themselves, yet whether that counts as knowing their own minds is still fiercely contested.
