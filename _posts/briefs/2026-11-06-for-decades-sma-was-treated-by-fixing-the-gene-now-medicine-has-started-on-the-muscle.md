For decades, spinal muscular atrophy was treated by fixing the gene. Now medicine has started on the muscle.

The FDA approved Isembyld (apitegromab-mstn) on 11 September, the first therapy for SMA that directly targets muscle loss. It is a monoclonal antibody that blocks myostatin, the body's own brake on muscle growth, and it is given alongside existing SMN2-targeted treatments — not instead of them — to adults and children aged two and over.

The evidence comes from the Phase 3 SAPPHIRE trial: 188 patients, 52 weeks, and a 2.2-point gain on the Hammersmith motor scale over placebo, with treated patients gaining function while those on SMN2 therapy alone declined. A third of treated patients improved by three points or more, against one in eight on placebo.

Conclusion: Isembyld is not a replacement for gene therapy, but rather its muscle — and for the first time, SMA treatment has both.
