Physicists have made the coldest thing ever. Aboard China’s Tiangong station, the Cold Atom Physics Rack cooled rubidium atoms to 9.7 picokelvins, less than ten-trillionths of a degree above absolute zero. That beats the 2021 record fourfold. The trick is weightlessness plus two nested laser traps, letting the atoms expand until they barely move. The prize is time: researchers can watch the cloud expand for seven seconds, long enough to test whether gravity tugs every kind of atom equally. The verdict is chillingly clear: the coldest place in the universe is a space station, and Einstein is next in line.
