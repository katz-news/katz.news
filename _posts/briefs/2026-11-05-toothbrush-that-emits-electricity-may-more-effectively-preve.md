Your toothbrush is about to get a shock. A good one. Piezoelectric plastic bristles generate electricity as they bend, making reactive-oxygen species that damage bacterial membranes as they scrub plaque away. Thicker bristles killed 53% of Streptococcus mutans and 78% of Porphyromonas gingivalis, against 9% and 8% for nylon. On cow enamel coated in human plaque, they removed over 99%, against about 80% for ordinary brushes. In a three-month trial of 109 adults, plaque, inflammation and bleeding fell roughly 40%, against about 15% for conventional brushes. The verdict is bristling brilliance: brushing may soon be a workout for bacteria.
